# Taskwatch: finishing and reviewing a session

The large clock and one-click controls already work well for timing one task. This pass focused on three decisions around the clock: what to start, what to save, and where the time went.

| Priority | Finding | Change and verification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Finish accurately | In the previous UI, opening **Stop at…** immediately could show a future-time error. The normal Stop button remained active above the correction form. | A native dialog identifies the task, keeps the background inactive, and previews the duration. The initial value is inside the session, including its first second; a paused session defaults to its pause time. Verified immediate open, an invalid cutoff, a corrected save, and Escape returning focus to the trigger. |
| Recognize success and recover | Pressing Enter in a blank field produced a page-wide error and a Retry button that reloaded history. Stopping reset the clock without a persistent indication of what was saved. | Blank-name guidance appears at the field. Read failures offer a reload; other messages can be dismissed. A successful stop shows the saved label and duration from the returned history. Verified blank Enter and matching preview, receipt, and history duration. |
| Read history in context | The count beside History described all completed sessions even in the weekly view. Dates repeated in same-day ranges; phone details repeated identical timestamps for uninterrupted sessions. | The count belongs to Sessions. Search can be cleared in place. Weekly groups show a date range, readable durations, and sessions with exact start/stop times. Phone details retain separate work intervals whenever they differ from the session span. Verified search focus, weekly expansion, and session details at a 360-pixel viewport. |

The first-use explanation remains in the empty history state. The footer links to privacy and storage guidance, including backup and recovery from a forgotten stop.

Evidence: local browser-mode exercises used sample history, including a running and paused task. The public and Node builds, type checks, and existing timer/store tests passed. These checks establish behavior and layout in the tested browser; they are not participant research or an assistive-technology audit.
